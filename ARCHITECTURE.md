# Traveling — GIS / Miles Architecture

## Modules
- **Interface (React 19)** — `src/main.jsx` mounts `src/App.jsx` in `#root`. State is one immutable object with subscribers (`src/state/store.js`, read with `useApp(selector)`); every change goes through `src/state/actions.js` (panels, selection, links, Esc), `src/state/route.js` (route preview) and `src/state/voice.js`. Singletons (stores, airports, globe, FX) live in `src/state/services.js`; focus and live announcements in `src/state/focus.js`. Miles, Planner and Discovery are `React.lazy`. `src/components/ErrorBoundary.jsx` isolates failures per panel.
- **Build** — `scripts/build-app.js` bundles `src/` and React into `app/` (committed); imports of `js/` stay external (`../js/…`), so validators and the app use the same files. `app/build.json` stores source/output hashes checked by `validate-react.js`; CI also rebuilds and compares.
- **Globe** — `src/components/Globe.jsx` gives the stage element to `MapEngine`; nothing inside the stage belongs to React.
- **Accessibility** — skip links, `src/components/Places.jsx` (text alternative to the globe), `Combobox.jsx` (ARIA combobox), focus moves to the opened panel's heading and returns to its opener. `scripts/test-a11y.js` walks the whole site with real key events and runs axe.
- `js/core/storage.js`: shared localStorage helpers (with `STORAGE_KEYS`).
- `src/state/route.js`: globe route preview; `js/planner/route.estimator.js`: route estimate shared by the Planner sections.
- `js/provenance.js`: one label per money figure (verified, live, Traveling estimate, calculated, unverified).
- `js/fx.service.js`: Banco Central do Brasil rates (PTAX API, then `data/fx-snapshot.json`).
- `js/history.loader.js`: loads the four history datasets with `import()` when the History tab opens or the browser is idle; they are not part of `COUNTRIES`.
- `js/planner/trip.model.js`, `js/planner/trip.store.js`: Planner trips (pure model with `PLANNER_STEPS`, `tripProgress`, the saved `step` and `entryReviewed`; validated by `scripts/validate-planner.js`) saved in localStorage under `traveling.trips`.
- Planner cities: step 1 has a city picker (top cities + accent-insensitive search) after the country; the trip stores `city` ({ id, name, lat, lng, region }, validated in `trip.model.js`). The flight estimate uses `AirportRepository.findGateway` (main airport within 150 km, else a standard-ICAO airport within 100 km, else the country gateway). New trips start on step 1; from the city card or Descoberta the city comes prefilled and the trip starts on step 2.
- `src/planner/` (React; `Planner.jsx`, `Wizard.jsx`, `steps/`, `sections/`): the Planner side panel. Six steps — Destino, Quando e com quem (month + duration chips; exact dates optional), Voo (payment, cabin, direct vs cheapest, with a read-only fare and miles estimate from `sections/FlightInfo.jsx`), Estilo (style, lodging, local transport, interests), Preparação, Resumo. Every answer is a tap on a choice card; the only text field is the country search. The choices live on the trip (`month`, `duration`, `group`, `payWith`, `flightPreference`, `style`, `transport`, `interests`) and drive `tripProgress`; older trips with typed expenses still count as done. The paragraph below describes the previous expense-line model, which `trip.model.js`, `planner.estimates.js` and `currency.js` still implement and validate but the UI no longer shows.
- Previous Planner UI (historical; the files named below no longer exist). A trip list plus a six-step flow: Destino, Datas, Voo, Orçamento, Preparação, Resumo. Each trip remembers its step; finished steps get a check from `tripProgress`. Step bodies for Voo and part of Orçamento come from `flight.section.js` (flight estimate; automatic cash vs miles comparison for LATAM Pass, Smiles and Azul Fidelidade with estimated miles, fees and effective cost; destination costs from reviewed Discovery profiles; math in `planner.estimates.js`). Destination costs split the per-person reference week into lodging (per room of two, or per bed for hostels, by lodging category) and daily spending (per person), with long-stay rates after 28 days. Traveling estimates keep their inputs (`source.basis`) and are recalculated, read-only, when dates, travelers, lodging or exchange rates change; marking one as Cotação or Pago turns it into an editable user value. Expenses can be typed in another currency: `currency.js` converts the original amount to BRL from the BCB snapshot or the day's PTAX (`fx.service.js` `resolveCurrencyRate`), always from the original value, never from an earlier conversion; Preparação comes from `prep.section.js` (entry summary with source, checklist progress shared with the drawer). Countries from the old "Quero ir" list (`traveling.wishlist`, read only) appear as destination ideas with ranges from `planner/ideas.js`.
- `js/map.engine.js`: Three.js globe, progressive world build, raycasting and camera. `zoom(factor)` scales the altitude for the + / − buttons; `minDistance` is 1.08 (≈500 km), close enough to read cities without diving into the low-resolution country geometry. `pickPlace`/`selectCity` route clicks on cities and state names before countries.
- `js/map.admin.layer.js` (rewritten): shows states, cities and urban outlines of ONE focus country — the selected city's country, else the selected country, else the country under the screen center (`countryAt` from the engine; over the sea the previous one stays). Performance techniques: only the focus country is loaded and drawn, with a 6-country LRU cache whose GPU buffers are disposed on eviction; urban outlines (`data/admin/urban/<ISO2>.json`, larger) are fetched only below distance 1.8 (shown and clickable below 1.6) or when a city with an outline is selected; the center country is recomputed only after the camera turns ~0.4°; label placement is dirty-checked (camera matrix + data/selection version), so a still globe does no label work; city dots are sorted by population so zoom only changes `drawRange`; picking scans only the focus country. Cities are picked by their area, not their dot: a visible name first, then the urban outline containing the surface point under the pointer (`MapEngine#surfacePoint` → point-in-polygon; when outlines overlap, the one whose city is nearest to the point), then — for cities without a mapped outline — an invisible circular target sized from population (≈2,500 hab/km², never under 12 px). Once a country is chosen (selected, or it is the focus country at distance ≤ 2.08), clicks inside it never select it again: every spot belongs to a city (`MapEngine#isExploring`). Pick order: a visible city name; the urban outline containing the point; a 12 px target around visible cities without an outline; otherwise the region (state/province, `data/admin/regions/<ISO2>.json`) containing the point selects its main city — capital, else regional seat, else the largest city inside it, or the nearest city when the region has none — and the whole region is the highlighted area (a state name does the same). The globe draws countries from world-atlas 1:110m while regions come from Natural Earth 1:10m, so coasts and borders leave strips inside the drawn country that belong to no region: a point there (confirmed with the same land-mesh test as country clicks, `pickCountry`) takes the nearest region (edge distance), or the nearest city in a country without regions, so every point of the chosen country belongs to a city and the sea never does. State names no longer outrank areas: a state label only counts where no region contains the point (the label is wide, overlaps the neighbour and hides when the hovered city name takes its place, which made hover and click disagree). A click at the spot of the last hover with the camera still selects exactly the hovered city and area (`MapEngine.hoverPick`). The surface point under the pointer is computed on the sphere where city shapes are drawn (`URBAN_RADIUS`, 1.0098), not on the ocean (1.0): with the ocean, the clickable area was shifted from the drawn one by parallax, by tens of pixels toward the screen edges when zoomed in. Highlights fade in by animating opacity; with `prefers-reduced-motion` the final opacity is applied at once (it used to be zeroed right after, which made the city area invisible). Clicking a city on the globe never moves the camera; selections from the Planner or Descoberta still fly to the city. Highlight geometries (holes supported, triangles subdivided to ~0.75° so large regions follow the curvature) are cached in a 24-entry LRU. A selected country also loads and shows its urban outlines right away, so city areas are clickable at the post-selection zoom. Hovering a city fills its whole area (a second highlight slot next to the selection) and always shows its name. Labels are zero-size anchors on the city point: the name is centered above it, the marker centered on it. The selected city's outline is drawn as a LineLoop plus a triangulated fill (contrasting color over a selected country).
- `js/geo/cities.js`: loads a country's cities without the 3D globe (Planner and Descoberta). City `id` (`BR-12`) is shared by the globe, the city card, the Planner and the Descoberta.
- Previous description of the zoom layer: Below distance 1.8 (or 2.15 for the selected country) it loads `data/admin/<ISO2>.json` for countries whose box intersects the visible cap, draws internal state borders as `LineSegments` clipped at the horizon, and places state and city names as HTML labels with greedy collision avoidance (max 90). Cities appear by population tier as the camera gets closer; national capitals and state seats come first. Every city (GeoNames ≥15k) also gets a WebGL dot; dots are sorted by population so zoom only changes `drawRange`. `pick(x, y)` returns the label under the pointer, else the nearest visible dot within 10 px. Clicking a city opens `src/components/CityCard.jsx` (state, country, population, local time from the IANA zone, coordinates; actions: zoom, country guide, plan); clicking a state name flies into it. At most 60 countries stay loaded; on-screen countries are never evicted.
- Visual layer: "liquid glass" tokens at the end of `styles.css` (`--glass-*` per theme) give the header, dock, panels, cards and buttons translucent blurred surfaces with a light edge; colors are the existing theme tokens. `prefers-reduced-transparency` falls back to solid surfaces.
- `js/geospatial.js`: authoritative WGS84 spherical transforms, Haversine and PIP.
- `js/countries.dataset.js`: 196-entry catalog (sovereign states plus Kosovo, Taiwan and French Guiana) + curated/regional enrichment.
- `js/country.exploration.js`: ISO flag identity and editorial eligibility for discovery, independent of WebGL.
- `js/checklist.engine.js`: compatible checklist groups, optional destination/season tips and real progress counts.
- `js/europe.entry.js`: single source of the European entry rules for Brazilian passports (Schengen, associated states, national regimes, EES/ETIAS sources).
- `js/europe.dataset.js` / `js/europe.priority.dataset.js`: legacy and priority layers for 20 European countries.
- `js/europe.complete.dataset.js`: final layer for all 45 European destinations; its entry records override the older layers.
- `js/landmarks.media.js`: static, attributed Wikimedia media linked to landmark identities.
- `src/components/MilesHub.jsx`: three-tab Milhas hub (Conversor, Cartões, Salas VIP). Calculations run on every input or chip tap; the milheiro reference comes from `AWARD_PROGRAMS` in `planner.estimates.js`. `milesRequest` in the store opens the lounges of an airport (used by the route preview).
- `js/miles.data.js`: educational paths and structured catalogs for card profiles, lounge types, upgrades, and 11 airport guides.
- `js/airports.repository.js`: offline core airports + lazy-loaded global catalog from `data/airports.json` (generated by `scripts/build-airports.js`).
- `js/pricing.engine.js`: distance/season/cabin award and cash estimations.
- `js/media.service.js`: serves only curated landmark media; never substitutes a generic photo.
- `backend/` (Cloudflare Worker, deployed at `traveling-api.travelingorgsenac.workers.dev`; live today: recently seen prices from Travelpayouts, flight/hotel search still on the `mock` provider): sits between the site and travel providers — routing, CORS, strict validation, rate limiting (global per-IP limit through the Cloudflare `SEARCH_LIMITER` binding, plus a per-instance in-memory minute/day counter), timeouts, partial results, short cache, circuit breaker, logs without personal data; only the declared-test `mock` provider. Contract and decisions: `docs/PHASE-3.1-TRAVEL-INTEGRATION-ARCHITECTURE.md`; details in `backend/README.md`.
- `js/api/travel.client.js`: site client for that backend, **off** unless `<meta name="traveling-api">` is present; builds search requests from a Planner trip.
- Page security: every HTML page carries one `<meta>` Content-Security-Policy, defined once in `scripts/csp.js` (applied by `npm run build:csp` to the hand-written pages and by `build:pages` to the generated ones). It allows scripts only from the site, the pinned `three@0.160.1` path on jsDelivr (never the whole CDN) and Cloudflare Web Analytics, plus the three inline scripts by sha256 hash (no `unsafe-inline`/`unsafe-eval`); data connections only to the site, jsDelivr, the BCB PTAX API, the backend and Cloudflare analytics. CDN files carry SRI (`integrity` on the preloads, the import map and the world-atlas `fetch` in `map.engine.js`). `topojson-client` is a local copy in `js/vendor/` because jsDelivr's `/+esm` builds are generated on demand and cannot use SRI. `validate-page.js` checks the CSP hashes and SRI coverage offline; `validate-sri.js` checks the hashes against the CDN. `validate-page.js` fails any page whose policy is stale. Live Server's injected auto-reload script is blocked by the CSP; `npm run dev` (`scripts/dev-server.js`) reloads through an external same-origin script instead and, locally only, also allows the dev backend on port 8787.
- `scripts/validate-*.js`: 30 read-only audits (see `README.md` for which ones need network).

## Country exploration at scale
`countries.dataset.js` remains the registry/aggregation layer. Future Africa, Oceania and Asia enrichments should follow the existing regional-module shape and join there, without changing `app.js` or `map.engine.js`. There is one reusable country drawer: selecting a country replaces its content; closing it removes its media. No country-specific drawer trees or landmark images are created at startup. `Descobrir` lists, inside an opened result, the cities of the itinerary region (within 250 km of its gateway airport, else the country's largest); tapping one highlights it on the globe and "Planejar" carries it into the Planner. `Descobrir` is one panel: interest, month, climate and budget chips on top and a live ranked list (up to 12) below; with no interest chosen it merges each country's best match across all interests. Tapping a result highlights the country and shows season bar, reasons and actions. The ranking engine (`destination.discovery.js`) is unchanged. It computes eligible keys once from curated entry, culture, checklist and verified-media fields, then uses the existing `selectCountry` and globe fly-to path. Haiti and Bahamas, whose entry records need review, are not eligible.

The selected flag image URL is derived from its ISO alpha-2 code and requested only for the opened country ([Flagcdn](https://flagcdn.com/)); a visible ISO fallback remains if it fails. The country name is the accessible heading. The primary landmark is requested on selection; secondary images receive their URL only near the drawer viewport through one replaceable `IntersectionObserver`. The existing media service remains the sole source of verified landmark files. Camera flights snap to the target when `prefers-reduced-motion` is enabled, without adding work to the WebGL frame loop.

The existing `checklist` groups and saved positional keys remain valid. A regional enrichment may optionally add `travelProfile` fields `moneyTips`, `healthTips`, `connectivityTips`, `transportTips`, `packingTips`, and `seasonalTips`. Tips are strings or `{ id, label, icon }`; seasonal entries use `{ id, months: [1..12], group, items }`. Only populated categories appear. The checklist lives only in the Planner's Preparação step and belongs to one trip: completed items are saved in `trip.checklist` (keys from `checklistGroups`), and seasonal tips follow the trip's departure month. Two trips to the same country keep separate progress. Stores from schema 1 are migrated once: each trip copies the items of its own country from the old per-country `traveling.checklist`, which is kept untouched. Progress counts the visible items' saved completion state; no live weather API or generic climate assumption is used.

The country drawer is for discovery (Visão geral, Cultura, História, states and cities). Entry rules (`src/planner/steps/PrepStep.jsx`, data from `entry.presentation.js`), the checklist and flight estimates are trip preparation and live in the Planner. "Planejar viagem" (country, city card, Estados, Descobrir) opens a confirmation in the Planner; nothing is created until the person confirms. The trip list has a ⋯ menu (Editar, Duplicar, Excluir); deleting asks for confirmation and offers "Desfazer" for 8 s. The X of the country drawer or of the Planner returns to the full globe (`resetToGlobe`); "Voltar" moves one step back inside the current flow. Camera flights (`MapEngine.flyTo`) are the only camera animation: a new flight replaces the running one from the current position, leftover drag inertia is discarded first, durations follow the travelled angle (`MOTION`), and reduced motion snaps directly.

## Antipodal click fix
The previous picker evaluated land meshes before determining the front intersection of the ocean sphere. A ray through open water can continue through the sphere and intersect land on the far side. The new picker first finds the nearest ocean-sphere intersection and rejects every land hit farther than that front surface (plus a small tolerance). Only then can a mesh be selected. If there is no front-side land mesh hit, the front-surface XYZ point is converted back to latitude/longitude and must pass strict antimeridian-safe point-in-polygon.

## Country border highlight
Base borders are the shared TopoJSON arcs (`#buildGlobalBorders`), drawn once for the whole world. A country gets its own outline only while it is selected, visited or highlighted by the visa-free filter; that outline is built lazily the first time and then just shown or hidden. Antimeridian cut edges (Russia, Fiji) are skipped so no false line appears at ±180°. WebGL lines are always 1 px wide; highlights differ by color and opacity only.

## Authoritative geographic frame
East-positive longitude, +Y north:
`x = R cos(lat) cos(lng)`
`y = R sin(lat)`
`z = -R cos(lat) sin(lng)`

Inverse:
`lat = asin(y / R)`
`lng = atan2(-z, x)`

All country meshes, borders, markers, fly-to camera targets, and fallback PIP use these same functions.

## Loading behavior

Startup loads the catalog, globe and drawer only. History (~160 KB), the Miles hub (~63 KB, plus its DOM) and Discovery (~60 KB) load with dynamic `import()` on first use, and are prefetched with `requestIdleCallback` once the interface is ready. Until the Miles hub loads, a placeholder keeps the latest route context. The globe renders on demand: `MapEngine.invalidate()` schedules frames only while something changes.

The shell is a single header (brand, search with `/` or Ctrl+K, Descobrir, Planner, Milhas, theme) plus a bottom dock (visited count, continents, visa-free filter). Icons are one inline SVG sprite in `index.html`. The brand reveal plays for about 1 s once per browser session. The dark-mode starfield is painted once per resize or theme change, not animated.

The application UI no longer waits for the world atlas. The interface becomes interactive first, while the globe reports progressive status. Network fetches have timeouts. A classic-script boot guard hides the global loader after 8 seconds even if ES-module/CDN loading fails before `app/main.js` executes.

## Performance and motion

Measured with long-task tracing in headless Chrome: after these changes, hover and drag produce no main-thread task over 50 ms (before: four 50–70 ms hitches per drag).

- **GPU:** the ocean uses `MeshStandardMaterial` (the physical material's 4% clearcoat was invisible and cost an extra lighting pass on the largest surface). Dynamic resolution: pixel ratio drops to 1 while the camera moves (drag, damping, flights, route preview) and returns to 1.5 about 200 ms after it stops (`MapEngine#setMotionResolution`). Shaders are compiled with `renderer.compileAsync` (parallel when `KHR_parallel_shader_compile` exists) before the globe is interactive.
- **Compositor:** large panels use a denser tint with a 14 px blur (they cover the moving globe; blur radius is the main cost). Closed panels get `visibility: hidden` after their exit transition, so off-screen glass is never composited. The country tooltip and globe labels move with `transform`, never `left`/`top`. Entrance animations that sit on centered elements animate the separate `translate` property so they compose with the centering transform.
- **Main thread:** globe labels are keyed by place (one element follows each city), fade in with the Web Animations API (opacity only) and are recycled; label placement is dirty-checked. Hovering a country prefetches its states/cities file in idle time (`AdminLayer#prefetch`), so selecting it shows states immediately.
- **Loading:** the browser uses the minified `three.module.min.js` (≈650 KB instead of 1.24 MB; validators still analyse the full build of the same version), and the page head preloads the world atlas, TopoJSON client and OrbitControls, so the country geometry arrives in ~0.2–0.6 s instead of ~1.8–2.0 s (measured).
- **Camera and light:** flights scale their duration with the arc they cover (never longer than requested; the route preview relies on that) and rise in a gentle arc on long hops; key and fill lights keep their offset from the camera (`#followCameraLights`), so every region is lit like the opening view; on narrow portrait screens the opening/home distance fits the whole globe (`#homeDistance`). Labels also fade out (Web Animations, 140 ms) before being recycled.
- **Motion system** (end of `styles.css`): shared easings (`--ease-out-expo`, `--ease-out-back`) and durations (`--dur-*`); panels glide and fade; panel contents rise in with a short stagger (first seven children); tabs, Planner steps and Descoberta results cross-fade; chips pop when selected; buttons lift on hover and compress on press; the city card animates in and out; country hover/selection colors and city/region highlights tween instead of switching (`MapEngine#tweenLand`, `AdminLayer#fadeTo`); `<Indicator>` (`src/components/ui.jsx`) slides one pill/underline under the active continent and tabs. Everything collapses to instant changes under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Media verification
The former Unsplash candidates were removed from production country data. Landmark identity (name, city, Wikidata ID, coordinates) is separate from static Wikimedia Commons media (file URL, attribution, license, source page, verification month). The media service does not fetch an API at runtime and does not show an unrelated fallback. If no entry has curated media, the drawer displays “Imagem ainda não verificada”. Legacy landmark identities without a complete review remain in the catalog as `needs-review`, without photos; they are not presented as verified imagery. The validator reports those separately from duplicate or incomplete-media errors.

## Miles & Airports data trust
The hub's three views are converter, cards and lounges. Upgrade methods and airport guides remain in `miles.data.js` (and are still validated) but are no longer shown. Lounges join the existing `AirportRepository` by IATA code.

Operational records use `status` (`verified`, `needs-review`, or `outdated`), `officialSource`, and `verifiedAt` (`YYYY-MM`). Product or airline-specific claims appear as current only when all three fields establish a verified official source. The catalogs now contain 8 card products, 5 lounge ecosystems, 6 airline upgrade examples, and 11 airport guides with 14 named lounge examples. Fields not established by official pages are omitted or explicitly listed in `uncertainFields`. Access remains conditional on the particular card, flight, terminal, visitor and issuer rules. The 3D globe and geographic picking modules are unchanged.

## Europe entry data
The regional dataset uses the existing country-enrichment schema. Common Schengen, UK and Ireland entry records are kept separate, with official source links and verification dates. EES and ETIAS are displayed as dated guidance, not permanent guarantees; travelers must confirm the current rule before booking or boarding. See `DATA_SOURCES.md` for provenance and remaining coverage gaps.
