# Traveling — GIS / Miles Architecture

## Modules
- `js/app.js`: application state, UI orchestration, search, country drawer, and on-demand loading of the Miles hub, Discovery and History.
- `js/core/storage.js`, `js/core/html.js`: shared localStorage helpers (with `STORAGE_KEYS`) and `escapeHtml`.
- `js/drawer/flights.tab.js`: Flights tab (airports, route estimate, globe route preview, dollar rate); exports `estimateRoute` and `exchangeRateLabel` for reuse.
- `js/provenance.js`: one label per money figure (verified, live, Traveling estimate, calculated, unverified).
- `js/fx.service.js`: Banco Central do Brasil rates (PTAX API, then `data/fx-snapshot.json`).
- `js/history.loader.js`: loads the four history datasets with `import()` when the History tab opens or the browser is idle; they are not part of `COUNTRIES`.
- `js/planner/trip.model.js`, `js/planner/trip.store.js`: Planner trips (pure model with `PLANNER_STEPS`, `tripProgress`, the saved `step` and `entryReviewed`; validated by `scripts/validate-planner.js`) saved in localStorage under `traveling.trips`.
- Planner cities: step 1 has a city picker (top cities + accent-insensitive search) after the country; the trip stores `city` ({ id, name, lat, lng, region }, validated in `trip.model.js`). The flight estimate uses `AirportRepository.findGateway` (main airport within 150 km, else a standard-ICAO airport within 100 km, else the country gateway). New trips start on step 1; from the city card or Descoberta the city comes prefilled and the trip starts on step 2.
- `js/planner/planner.ui.js` (rewritten 2026-11): the Planner side panel. Six steps — Destino, Quando e com quem (month + duration chips; exact dates optional), Voo (payment, cabin, direct vs cheapest, with a read-only fare and miles estimate from `flight.section.js`), Estilo (style, lodging, local transport, interests), Preparação, Resumo. Every answer is a tap on a choice card; the only text field is the country search. The choices live on the trip (`month`, `duration`, `group`, `payWith`, `flightPreference`, `style`, `transport`, `interests`) and drive `tripProgress`; older trips with typed expenses still count as done. The paragraph below describes the previous expense-line model, which `trip.model.js`, `planner.estimates.js` and `currency.js` still implement and validate but the UI no longer shows.
- Previous Planner UI (loaded on first open). A trip list plus a six-step flow: Destino, Datas, Voo, Orçamento, Preparação, Resumo. Each trip remembers its step; finished steps get a check from `tripProgress`. Step bodies for Voo and part of Orçamento come from `flight.section.js` (flight estimate; automatic cash vs miles comparison for LATAM Pass, Smiles and Azul Fidelidade with estimated miles, fees and effective cost; destination costs from reviewed Discovery profiles; math in `planner.estimates.js`). Destination costs split the per-person reference week into lodging (per room of two, or per bed for hostels, by lodging category) and daily spending (per person), with long-stay rates after 28 days. Traveling estimates keep their inputs (`source.basis`) and are recalculated, read-only, when dates, travelers, lodging or exchange rates change; marking one as Cotação or Pago turns it into an editable user value. Expenses can be typed in another currency: `currency.js` converts the original amount to BRL from the BCB snapshot or the day's PTAX (`fx.service.js` `resolveCurrencyRate`), always from the original value, never from an earlier conversion; Preparação comes from `prep.section.js` (entry summary with source, checklist progress shared with the drawer). Countries from the old "Quero ir" list (`traveling.wishlist`, read only) appear as destination ideas with ranges from `planner/ideas.js`.
- `js/map.engine.js`: Three.js globe, progressive world build, raycasting and camera. `zoom(factor)` scales the altitude for the + / − buttons; `minDistance` is 1.08 (≈500 km), close enough to read cities without diving into the low-resolution country geometry. `pickPlace`/`selectCity` route clicks on cities and state names before countries.
- `js/map.admin.layer.js` (rewritten): shows states, cities and urban outlines of ONE focus country — the selected city's country, else the selected country, else the country under the screen center (`countryAt` from the engine; over the sea the previous one stays). Performance techniques: only the focus country is loaded and drawn, with a 6-country LRU cache whose GPU buffers are disposed on eviction; urban outlines (`data/admin/urban/<ISO2>.json`, larger) are fetched only below distance 1.45 or when a city with an outline is selected; the center country is recomputed only after the camera turns ~0.4°; label placement is dirty-checked (camera matrix + data/selection version), so a still globe does no label work; city dots are sorted by population so zoom only changes `drawRange`; picking scans only the focus country. Labels are zero-size anchors on the city point: the name is centered above it, the marker centered on it. The selected city's outline is drawn as a LineLoop plus a triangulated fill (contrasting color over a selected country).
- `js/geo/cities.js`: loads a country's cities without the 3D globe (Planner and Descoberta). City `id` (`BR-12`) is shared by the globe, the city card, the Planner and the Descoberta.
- Previous description of the zoom layer: Below distance 1.8 (or 2.15 for the selected country) it loads `data/admin/<ISO2>.json` for countries whose box intersects the visible cap, draws internal state borders as `LineSegments` clipped at the horizon, and places state and city names as HTML labels with greedy collision avoidance (max 90). Cities appear by population tier as the camera gets closer; national capitals and state seats come first. Every city (GeoNames ≥15k) also gets a WebGL dot; dots are sorted by population so zoom only changes `drawRange`. `pick(x, y)` returns the label under the pointer, else the nearest visible dot within 10 px. Clicking a city opens `js/city.card.js` (state, country, population, local time from the IANA zone, coordinates; actions: zoom, country guide, plan); clicking a state name flies into it. At most 60 countries stay loaded; on-screen countries are never evicted.
- Visual layer: "liquid glass" tokens at the end of `styles.css` (`--glass-*` per theme) give the header, dock, panels, cards and buttons translucent blurred surfaces with a light edge; colors are the existing theme tokens. `prefers-reduced-transparency` falls back to solid surfaces.
- `js/geospatial.js`: authoritative WGS84 spherical transforms, Haversine and PIP.
- `js/countries.dataset.js`: 196-entry catalog (sovereign states plus Kosovo, Taiwan and French Guiana) + curated/regional enrichment.
- `js/country.exploration.js`: ISO flag identity and editorial eligibility for discovery, independent of WebGL.
- `js/checklist.engine.js`: compatible checklist groups, optional destination/season tips and real progress counts.
- `js/europe.entry.js`: single source of the European entry rules for Brazilian passports (Schengen, associated states, national regimes, EES/ETIAS sources).
- `js/europe.dataset.js` / `js/europe.priority.dataset.js`: legacy and priority layers for 20 European countries.
- `js/europe.complete.dataset.js`: final layer for all 45 European destinations; its entry records override the older layers.
- `js/landmarks.media.js`: static, attributed Wikimedia media linked to landmark identities.
- `js/miles.engine.js`: three-tab Milhas hub (Conversor, Cartões, Salas VIP). Calculations run on every input or chip tap; the milheiro reference comes from `AWARD_PROGRAMS` in `planner.estimates.js`. `showAirport(code)` opens the lounges of that airport (used by the route preview).
- `js/miles.data.js`: educational paths and structured catalogs for card profiles, lounge types, upgrades, and 11 airport guides.
- `js/airports.repository.js`: offline core airports + lazy-loaded global catalog from `data/airports.json` (generated by `scripts/build-airports.js`).
- `js/pricing.engine.js`: distance/season/cabin award and cash estimations.
- `js/media.service.js`: serves only curated landmark media; never substitutes a generic photo.
- `scripts/validate-*.js`: 18 read-only audits (see `README.md` for which ones need network).

## Country exploration at scale
`countries.dataset.js` remains the registry/aggregation layer. Future Africa, Oceania and Asia enrichments should follow the existing regional-module shape and join there, without changing `app.js` or `map.engine.js`. There is one reusable country drawer: selecting a country replaces its content; closing it removes its media. No country-specific drawer trees or landmark images are created at startup. `Descobrir` lists, inside an opened result, the cities of the itinerary region (within 250 km of its gateway airport, else the country's largest); tapping one highlights it on the globe and "Planejar" carries it into the Planner. `Descobrir` is one panel: interest, month, climate and budget chips on top and a live ranked list (up to 12) below; with no interest chosen it merges each country's best match across all interests. Tapping a result highlights the country and shows season bar, reasons and actions. The ranking engine (`destination.discovery.js`) is unchanged. It computes eligible keys once from curated entry, culture, checklist and verified-media fields, then uses the existing `selectCountry` and globe fly-to path. Haiti and Bahamas, whose entry records need review, are not eligible.

The selected flag image URL is derived from its ISO alpha-2 code and requested only for the opened country ([Flagcdn](https://flagcdn.com/)); a visible ISO fallback remains if it fails. The country name is the accessible heading. The primary landmark is requested on selection; secondary images receive their URL only near the drawer viewport through one replaceable `IntersectionObserver`. The existing media service remains the sole source of verified landmark files. Camera flights snap to the target when `prefers-reduced-motion` is enabled, without adding work to the WebGL frame loop.

The existing `checklist` groups and saved positional keys remain valid. A regional enrichment may optionally add `travelProfile` fields `moneyTips`, `healthTips`, `connectivityTips`, `transportTips`, `packingTips`, and `seasonalTips`. Tips are strings or `{ id, label, icon }`; seasonal entries use `{ id, months: [1..12], group, items }`. Only populated categories appear. The trip-month selector in Flights and the Checklist month selector share one value when seasonal tips exist. Progress counts the current visible item's saved completion state; no live weather API or generic climate assumption is used.

## Antipodal click fix
The previous picker evaluated land meshes before determining the front intersection of the ocean sphere. A ray through open water can continue through the sphere and intersect land on the far side. The new picker first finds the nearest ocean-sphere intersection and rejects every land hit farther than that front surface (plus a small tolerance). Only then can a mesh be selected. If there is no front-side land mesh hit, the front-surface XYZ point is converted back to latitude/longitude and must pass strict antimeridian-safe point-in-polygon.

## Country border highlight
Base borders are the shared TopoJSON arcs (`#buildGlobalBorders`), drawn once for the whole world. A country gets its own outline only while it is selected, visited or highlighted by the visa-free filter; that outline is built lazily the first time and then just shown or hidden. Antimeridian cut edges (Russia, Fiji) are skipped so no false line appears at ±180°. WebGL lines are always 1 px wide; highlights differ by color and opacity only.

## Authoritative geographic frame
East-positive longitude, +Y north:
`x = R cos(lat) cos(lng)`
`y = R sin(lat)`
`z = -R cos(lat) sin(lng)`

Inverse:
`lat = asin(y / R)`
`lng = atan2(-z, x)`

All country meshes, borders, markers, fly-to camera targets, and fallback PIP use these same functions.

## Loading behavior

Startup loads the catalog, globe and drawer only. History (~160 KB), the Miles hub (~63 KB, plus its DOM) and Discovery (~60 KB) load with dynamic `import()` on first use, and are prefetched with `requestIdleCallback` once the interface is ready. Until the Miles hub loads, a placeholder keeps the latest route context. The globe renders on demand: `MapEngine.invalidate()` schedules frames only while something changes.

The shell is a single header (brand, search with `/` or Ctrl+K, Descobrir, Planner, Milhas, theme) plus a bottom dock (visited count, continents, visa-free filter). Icons are one inline SVG sprite in `index.html`. The brand reveal plays for about 1 s once per browser session. The dark-mode starfield is painted once per resize or theme change, not animated.

The application UI no longer waits for the world atlas. The interface becomes interactive first, while the globe reports progressive status. Network fetches have timeouts. A classic-script boot guard hides the global loader after 8 seconds even if ES-module/CDN loading fails before `app.js` executes.

## Media verification
The former Unsplash candidates were removed from production country data. Landmark identity (name, city, Wikidata ID, coordinates) is separate from static Wikimedia Commons media (file URL, attribution, license, source page, verification month). The media service does not fetch an API at runtime and does not show an unrelated fallback. If no entry has curated media, the drawer displays “Imagem ainda não verificada”. Legacy landmark identities without a complete review remain in the catalog as `needs-review`, without photos; they are not presented as verified imagery. The validator reports those separately from duplicate or incomplete-media errors.

## Miles & Airports data trust
The hub's three views are converter, cards and lounges. Upgrade methods and airport guides remain in `miles.data.js` (and are still validated) but are no longer shown. Lounges join the existing `AirportRepository` by IATA code.

Operational records use `status` (`verified`, `needs-review`, or `outdated`), `officialSource`, and `verifiedAt` (`YYYY-MM`). Product or airline-specific claims appear as current only when all three fields establish a verified official source. The catalogs now contain 8 card products, 5 lounge ecosystems, 6 airline upgrade examples, and 11 airport guides with 14 named lounge examples. Fields not established by official pages are omitted or explicitly listed in `uncertainFields`. Access remains conditional on the particular card, flight, terminal, visitor and issuer rules. The 3D globe and geographic picking modules are unchanged.

## Europe entry data
The regional dataset uses the existing country-enrichment schema. Common Schengen, UK and Ireland entry records are kept separate, with official source links and verification dates. EES and ETIAS are displayed as dated guidance, not permanent guarantees; travelers must confirm the current rule before booking or boarding. See `DATA_SOURCES.md` for provenance and remaining coverage gaps.
